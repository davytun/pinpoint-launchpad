Pinpoint Launchpad

How the platform flows

Three roles, one mediated pipeline. Pinpoint and Investor Relations sit between Founder and Investor on every engagement: the Founder gives consent, IR makes the final operational decision, and the system records each state. Nothing reaches one side without passing through the desks.

02

Investor engagement

One venture, three independent tracks. A pending or approved track never blocks another; each keeps its own state from request to resolution. Founder authorization is consent for Pinpoint to proceed — it is not a grant of access, an introduction, or a disclosure of contacts. IR makes the final decision on every track.

Post-introduction diligence

The loop the whole mediation exists for: founder input and internal notes stay private until IR explicitly releases an investor-visible response. 04

Core invariants

Rules the platform enforces in middleware, request classes, services, and tests — not just in the interface.

R1

Mediation is absolute

There is no direct Founder–Investor channel anywhere: no private contact disclosure, no direct messaging. Every arrow between the two sides passes through a Pinpoint desk.

R2

Consent is not access

Founder authorization permits Pinpoint and IR to proceed. It never grants a data room, never books a call, and never releases information. IR's decision is the operational trigger.

R3

Engagements are independent

Question, call, and data room run as parallel tracks on the same venture. Each has its own lifecycle; approving, declining, or revoking one never changes another.

R4

Revoke is not cancel

Pulling data room access leaves an approved or scheduled introduction untouched. Reinstate notifies the investor explicitly.

R5

Diligence is earned, not menued

The follow-up loop opens only after a completed, IR-coordinated introduction — enforced server-side, with the founder's words released only after IR sanitizes them.

R6

Sensitive data has one door

KYC evidence stays with Compliance. Confidential documents stay data-room-only, behind approved KYC, an active grant, and signed URLs. Hiding a UI element is never the authorization boundary.
